• We posit that frontier agentic models can capture both the semantic intent and physical requirements of a motion for generalizable humanoid retargeting.
  • Closed-loop simulation feedback lets LLM agents iteratively refine motion and control, scored by object-tracking error and an LLM judge.
  • Agentic swarm vs. longer iterative refinement. A four-agent swarm beats a single Codex agent by 20% under a fixed time budget, but compute-matched Codex surpasses it by 7.5% at 4x the time.
  • Experience from related tasks. Prior experience, stored as summaries of strategies and outcomes, cuts object-tracking error on a new humanoid-object task from 23.35 to 7.77 cm at equal compute and time.

How the trace audit was counted
  • Independent overlap: at least two workers reported testing the same type of change. The audit covers motion timing, finger closure or stiffness, and object-pose feedback. It does not count identical programs or measure wasted trials.
  • Controller reuse: a peer’s controller file was received, copied locally and used in a completed simulation. The saved execution command and completion record verify the test.
  • Division of experiments: an explicit allocation of different tests was followed by reported outcomes from both workers.
  • Response to critique: a specific peer observation was followed by a change to a test, candidate selection or conclusion. The change need not improve the result.

All 40 paired tasks were included. Readable independent logs cover 158 of 160 workers; collaborative logs cover all 160. Ten HUMOTO independent runs have a 15-minute four-worker stage followed by a single-worker continuation; only the four-worker stage is included in this audit. Reporting detail and run duration affect what can be verified.

The logs establish reuse and coordination, but do not establish that communication reduces repeated work. Unverified cases are not counted as absent. The outcome comparison does not isolate the contribution of each communication behavior.
