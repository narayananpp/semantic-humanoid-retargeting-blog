• Existing human-to-humanoid retargeting methods optimize kinematic alignment, which does not guarantee that a motion’s semantic intent is preserved under physical execution. We posit that frontier agentic models can capture both the semantic intent and physical requirements of a motion for generalizable retargeting.
  • Closed loop simulation feedback allows LLM agents to iteratively refine the robot motion and control. We evaluate humanoid retargeting through object-tracking errors and semantic scoring (LLM-as-Judge) to measure motion fidelity and completion of the intended task.
  • We study the compute-time tradeoff between deploying an agentic swarm and longer single-agent iterative refinement. Under a fixed time budget, a four-agent swarm outperforms a single Codex agent by 20% in LLM-scored semantic success rate, but a compute-matched Codex agent surpasses the swarm by 7.5%, at 4x the time.
  • Experience from related tasks, stored as summaries of tested strategies and their outcomes, improves success on new tasks. On a novel humanoid-object task, prior furniture-moving experience reduces mean object-tracking error from 23.35 cm to 7.77 cm and turns a semantic FAIL into a PASS at equal compute and time.
